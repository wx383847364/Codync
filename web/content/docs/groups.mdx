A group chat puts several of your bots and you in one conversation. Each member answers in turn, reads what the others said, and does its work with its own agent, folder, tools and approval setting. Use a group when you want bots to discuss a problem, review each other's work, or split a task while you watch.

A group is a room for bots you already have. Each member uses its own agent and folder.

## Create a group

- **iPhone:** on the Bots screen, tap **+** and choose **New group chat**.
- **Desktop:** click **+** above your bots and choose **New group chat**.
- **Terminal UI:** press `m`.

Pick the bots to add, type a name if you like, and choose **Create**. Without a name, the group is named after its members ("Alice, Bob").

- A group needs at least one bot.
- A group can't contain another group.
- If an ordinary group with exactly the same bots already exists, this opens that group.

Use **Create new group session** for a fresh chat with your existing bots. Use **Create next stage group** for a new team of bot instances with a designated lead. To continue from a stage group, accept its handoff in **Stage details**, then select **Use accepted handoff** in the creation form.

## Start a fresh conversation with the same group

- **Desktop:** right-click the group in the sidebar and choose **Create new group session**.
- **iPhone and Android:** touch and hold the group in the bot list and choose **Create new group session**.
- **Terminal UI:** select the group and press `Ctrl+K` and choose **Create new group session**.

The new group opens immediately with the same members in the same order. Its name gets the next number, such as **Release 1**, **Release 2**, or **Release 3**. Each member starts a fresh agent session for that group, and later messages in that group use its own conversation. Bots still use their own settings, workspace and long-term memory.

## Start the next stage of a task

Open the source group's menu and choose **Create next stage group**. Right-click on desktop, touch and hold on iPhone or Android, or use `Ctrl+K` in the terminal. Name the stage, enter the goal and acceptance criteria, and select the roles you need. Review their instructions for stable role requirements and choose a stage lead.

The new group starts with fresh bot instances and a draft containing the goal and acceptance criteria. Review the draft and send it when you are ready. New bots get their own workspace by default. You can explicitly reuse an existing project directory for a role. Codync shared memory starts off. The form identifies agents that keep their own shared memory.

Messages without an explicit recipient go to the stage lead. Use `@` to select another member, reply to a member's message, or use `@all` to address everyone. Keep work and revisions for the same stage in this group.

### Save a handoff and close a stage

Choose **Stage details** from the stage group's menu. Edit and save the Markdown handoff, add durable evidence references, and choose **Accept stage** when you have verified the result. Check the delivery statuses for failed, cancelled, or pending work.

For files from an earlier bot's workspace, first share them in its stage group. Select them under **Shared evidence files** to pin their saved versions. The next stage receives its own copy of each selected file.

Choose **Close stage** when its active queue is empty. You can keep reading its messages and handoff. **Reopen stage** lets you send work there again. When creating the next stage, select **Use accepted handoff** to carry its saved version into your first task. **Restore first draft** recovers the goal and acceptance criteria before your first send.

### View stage usage

Open **Stage details** and choose **Stage usage**. Compare deliveries, execution attempts, ACP prompts, new sessions and agent process starts. Token totals include the fields each model reported and show how many attempts reported each field. Missing fields are shown as unavailable. Account usage stays in the existing **Usage** screen.

## Edit, remove members, delete

Open the group's settings to change it:

- **iPhone:** tap the group's name at the top of the chat to edit the group.
- **Desktop:** click the group's name at the top of the chat. The details panel lists the members, with **Add Member** and an X to remove each one.
- **Terminal UI:** select the group and press `e`.

In the editor you can rename the group, add or remove members, and fill in **What this group works on**. Every member reads that line, so use it to say what the room is for.

**Delete group chat** removes the group and its conversation. Its bots stay. If you delete a bot, it leaves every group it was in.

## Who answers

When you send a message, the group runs a round of answers:

1. **Who is asked.** Selected or uniquely named members, plus the author you reply to. `@all` and `@everyone` ask everyone. With no target, a stage group asks its lead and other groups ask everyone.
2. **One at a time per message.** Available members take turns so each can see prior replies. If a member is still working on an earlier message, even in this group, the other recipients can answer first. The busy member keeps each request queued and receives its original message when free.
3. **Passing.** A member with nothing to add can pass without a message. A pass at the end keeps a result the member already gave in that turn, provided it did not continue tool work. Tool-assigned work that passes without a result shows that it provided no result.
4. **Continue until handoff.** Members keep doing authorized work and assigning the next action until they deliver the result to you or need your decision, information or approval. Group work has no fixed round, reply-count or handoff-depth cutoff. After ten minutes, the next recipient of the same message can take a turn while the earlier work continues. Busy members keep their accepted work queued. You can still stop the group yourself.

Above the message box, each member working in this group has a separate activity label
with its name, current activity and elapsed time. As members finish, their labels clear individually.

Each member's reply is the one message the room sees. Its tool calls, thoughts and approval cards for that turn appear in the group. The member's own chat doesn't show them. Open **Full conversation** to see its steps.

### @-mentions

Type `@` at the cursor to see members. Pick one by click or tap; the up/down keys cycle through the list in either direction, wrapping between the first and last member. Enter or Tab inserts the selected member, and Esc closes the list. Selection preserves the text after the cursor and never sends. Chinese input-method confirmation stays with the input method. Same-name members show an ID hint. Selected identities survive drafts, offline sends, retries and later renames.

You can also type a full name, name without spaces or first word. Ambiguous names ask you to choose again and keep the draft; unknown `@word` remains text. Mentions inside code, quoted lines, email and links do not assign work.
Replying to a member's message counts as mentioning it. To reply, long-press the message on iPhone, use **Reply** below the message on the desktop, or press `r` in the terminal UI.

### Reactions and files

You can react to messages in a group. Members see your reactions on the messages they're shown, and they can react too.

Files you attach in a group are available to every member.

## Queued work and recovery

Delivery cards start as a single status line. Expand one to see each member's status,
the delivery attempt and any retry action. The original message stays in its chat bubble
and is not repeated inside the card. In the terminal, select the card and press `o` to
show or hide its details. Historical cards cancelled by an older relay limit keep their original reason;
this leaves the replies already sent in the chat.

A busy member keeps each accepted request queued and reads the room again when free.
New messages never replace accepted work. Cards show each target's state and retry attempt;
failed or interrupted work offers a retry action in its details, which preserves the old record.

Group **Stop**, removing a member or deleting the group cancels its unfinished work.
Stopping a private chat preserves requests queued by other groups. After a restart, work
that may already have started is marked interrupted with an uncertain outcome and waits
for your decision to retry. Check for external effects before retrying uncertain work.

If results cannot be saved, they stay in memory and the member pauses. **Retry save** is
available beside the affected member in the source group and in its own chat, even if a card could not be written. The host retries saving
five times at three-second intervals first. If storage stays unavailable and the process
exits, unsaved results may be lost; Codync reports uncertainty and does not rerun them.

## Starting a new session

`/new` waits for the current task and result save before switching; `/new-now` stops and
switches after interruption has been saved; `/new-cancel` cancels the pending switch.
A member's private chat and ordinary groups share the main session. Groups created with **Create new group session** have a separate session for each member.


## What each member remembers

- **Session history.** A member shares its main session between its private chat and ordinary groups. A numbered group created with **Create new group session** has its own conversation history and member sessions.
- **Complete request.** Each turn receives the original request, attachments and quote, plus recent room context. Prior replies are available to the next member.
- **Private chats.** In an ordinary group, the shared session still holds the member's private conversation, and members are instructed to keep it private.
- **Long-term memory.** Group work does not feed user-conversation dreaming. A tool assignment is never recorded as the user's own words.

## Notifications and voice

Replies appear in the group as they arrive. The group sends a done notification when it finishes its current work, so overlapping exchanges can share a notification. Late replies can send their own notifications. Approval notifications open the group.
Voice calls work in groups too: each reply is read with its speaker's name. See [Voice](/docs/voice).

To collaborate without a shared room, see [Teamwork](/docs/teamwork).

## Long tasks and context

Members keep the original task, later user messages, attachment and mention identities,
quotes and unfinished plans. They receive a small set of progress excerpts and can read
older group originals when needed, rather than receiving the entire room history on every
turn. Excerpts never replace your original requirements or mark pending work complete.

Supported agents can organize their existing session between assignments. The group shows
only a short status; private session summaries are not posted into the group. Collaboration
continues until delivery or your input is needed, without a fixed number of handoffs.
When reported context shows too little space for the full task, the request stays queued with a
notice: choose a larger-window model or intentionally start a new session. Token estimates
are approximate; large original tasks and the agents' own tools can still cost more.
